1What you will learn

  • Where personal and confidential data flows in a RAG system — including places people forget.
  • Core principles: minimization, purpose, access, retention, deletion and audit.
  • How to delete a document completely.
  • The regulations to be aware of (GDPR, India's DPDP Act, the EU AI Act), at a high level.

2Why it matters

RAG copies data into many places: object storage, a database, two search indexes, caches, logs and traces. Every copy must follow the same rules. A "deleted" document that still answers questions from an old index, or sits in a trace, is a privacy incident. Running models locally is a big advantage here: data never leaves your infrastructure.

Principles

Minimize

Ingest and log only what is needed. Redact PII that isn't needed for answers.

Purpose

Use data only for the purpose it was collected for. Don't train on it without a basis.

Access

Role- or attribute-based access, enforced at retrieval (lesson 23).

Retention

Defined lifetimes for documents, chat history, logs and traces, with automatic cleanup.

Deletion

Remove every copy, including derived ones, within a defined time.

Audit

Who uploaded, viewed, changed, approved or deleted — kept tamper-evident.

4How it works: complete deletion

  1. Mark the document DELETED in PostgreSQL. Retrieval stops returning it immediately (status filter).
  2. Emit outbox delete events: remove its points from Qdrant and documents from OpenSearch in every retained index generation.
  3. Invalidate caches that may contain its text or answers based on it.
  4. After lifecycle checks (no published generation references it, retention passed), delete MinIO objects and their versions.
  5. Purge or redact its text from logs and traces according to policy; let backups age out.
  6. Record the deletion in audit_events.

PII handling

  • Detect PII during ingestion (e.g. Presidio, plus regex for national ID formats) and tag or redact it according to policy.
  • Mask PII in logs and traces by default; give raw access only to a small, audited group.
  • Pseudonymize user identifiers in analytics.

5In the real world: regulations to know (not legal advice)

  • GDPR (EU): lawful basis, data minimization, rights of access and erasure, data protection by design.
  • DPDP Act 2023 (India): consent and notice, purpose limitation, data principal rights (including erasure), security safeguards and breach reporting.
  • EU AI Act: risk-based obligations and transparency duties, phased in from 2025 onward.
  • Sector rules may apply: health, finance, government.

Involve your legal or compliance team early. Good engineering — clear lineage, complete deletion, access control, audit — makes compliance much easier.

6Common mistakes

  • Deleting from the database but not from the vector and search indexes.
  • Forgetting old index generations kept for rollback.
  • Full prompts with personal data in long-lived traces.
  • Treating embeddings as "anonymous numbers".

7In our platform

Where this lives

  • Lifecycle: documents.lifecycle_status and document_versions.status (SUPERSEDED, DELETED); deletion invalidates both indexes before MinIO cleanup.
  • storage_objects prevents deleting bytes still referenced elsewhere; audit_events records every action.
  • Trace policy: Langfuse spans carry IDs, timings and token counts by default; text only where policy allows.
  • Fully local models: no document or question leaves the deployment.

8Check yourself

Why are embeddings considered sensitive?
Research shows text can be partly reconstructed from embeddings, so they carry the sensitivity of the original text.
List the places a deleted document may still exist.
Vector index(es), lexical index(es), old index generations, caches, logs and traces, object storage versions and backups.
