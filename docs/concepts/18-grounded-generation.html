1What you will learn

  • How to turn ranked chunks into a good context: selection, expansion, dedupe, ordering, budget.
  • The "lost in the middle" effect, and how to work around it.
  • Citation formats that can be checked automatically.
  • When and how to abstain, and how to validate an answer before returning it.

2Why it matters

This is where retrieval meets generation. Even with perfect retrieval, a cluttered context, missing source labels or no permission to say "I don't know" produce hallucinated or uncheckable answers. Grounded generation means every claim is supported by a cited source, or the system says it cannot answer.

3Core ideas

Context assembly

  1. Select the top-n reranked chunks, plus any above a relevance threshold.
  2. Expand where needed: swap a child chunk for its parent section, or add neighbouring chunks (lesson 11).
  3. Dedupe overlapping text and near-identical chunks from different versions; prefer the current version.
  4. Order: most relevant first, or group chunks by document in document order for readability.
  5. Fit the budget: count tokens, drop the weakest chunks first, and reserve answer tokens (lesson 02).
  6. Label each source with a number and metadata: title, section, page, date.

Lost in the middle

Studies found that models use information at the beginning and end of a long context better than information in the middle. Practical consequences:

  • Fewer, better chunks beat many mediocre ones. Reranking and thresholds matter.
  • Put the strongest evidence first, and repeat the question or key instruction at the end of a long prompt.
  • Test your model: newer long-context models are better at this, but rarely perfect.

4How it works: citations, abstention and validation

Citations

Sources
[1] Leave Policy v3 · §4.2 Carry-over · p.6
    "Employees may carry over up to five days of unused annual leave…"
[2] HR FAQ 2026 · "Leave" · p.2
    "Carried-over days must be used by 31 March."

Answer
You can carry over up to five unused annual-leave days [1], and they must be used by 31 March [2].
  • Cite at the sentence or claim level, with numbers that map back to chunk IDs in your database.
  • Optionally store quoted spans so the UI can highlight the exact supporting text.

Abstention

Give the system an explicit, normal path for "not enough evidence":

  • Before generation: no candidate above the reranker threshold → don't call the LLM; abstain or ask a clarifying question.
  • During generation: the prompt allows "answerable": false with a reason.
  • After generation: validation fails → repair once, otherwise abstain.

Hallucination types to watch

  • Fabrication: facts not in any source.
  • Misattribution: a correct fact with the wrong citation.
  • Over-generalization: "always" when the source says "usually".
  • Conflation: merging two sources, or two versions, that disagree.

5In the real world

  • When sources conflict, prefer the newest valid version — or state the conflict with both citations rather than picking silently.
  • Show citations as clickable links that open the page in a document viewer. Users trust what they can check.
  • Measure abstention: both false abstentions (the answer was there) and missed abstentions (the system answered without evidence).
  • Keep the context focused: 3–8 good chunks is typical for factual questions.

6Common mistakes

  • Stuffing the context window "because it fits".
  • Citations that are just document names, with no page or section.
  • Never abstaining, so the model guesses whenever retrieval fails.
  • Not checking that cited sources were actually in the context.

8Check yourself

Why can adding more chunks make answers worse?
Noise and conflicting passages distract the model, important facts can land in the middle where they're used less, and the cost and latency rise.
Name three checks to run on an answer before showing it.
Schema validity; citation references exist and were in the context; claims are supported by the cited text (plus safety checks).
When should the system abstain without calling the LLM at all?
When no retrieved candidate passes the calibrated relevance threshold — there is no evidence to ground an answer.
