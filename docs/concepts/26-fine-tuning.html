2Why it matters

Fine-tuning is often proposed first and needed last. Done for the wrong reason, it costs weeks and makes knowledge stale. Done for the right reason, it gives a smaller, cheaper model that follows your format perfectly, or a retriever that finally understands your jargon.

3Core ideas

The adaptation ladder — climb only as far as needed

  1. Prompting: clearer instructions, examples, schema (lesson 07).
  2. RAG for knowledge (lesson 08).
  3. Better retrieval: chunking, hybrid search, reranking (lessons 11–17).
  4. Fine-tune the embedder and/or reranker on domain query-passage pairs.
  5. Fine-tune the LLM for behaviour, format or cost.

4How it works

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning

  • LoRA: freeze the model; train small low-rank matrices added to some layers (often under 1% of the parameters). The adapters are small files, swappable per task.
  • QLoRA: LoRA on top of a 4-bit quantized base model, so 7–14B models can be tuned on a single GPU.
  • Full fine-tuning: update all weights. Most expensive; rarely needed for applications.

Preference tuning and distillation

  • DPO (and related methods): train on pairs of better vs worse answers to shift behaviour without a separate reward model.
  • Distillation: generate high-quality outputs with a large model (check its license), then fine-tune a small model to imitate them.

Fine-tuning retrievers — the RAG sweet spot

  1. Collect (query, relevant chunk) pairs from evaluation cases, user clicks and reviewed answers — or generate synthetic questions per chunk with a local LLM.
  2. Mine hard negatives: chunks that the current retriever ranks high but are wrong.
  3. Train with a contrastive loss (sentence-transformers makes this a short script); train rerankers on (query, passage, label).
  4. Evaluate on a held-out set: recall@k, MRR, nDCG.
  5. A new embedder → re-embed everything into a new index generation; a new reranker only changes query-time behaviour.

5In the real world

  • Data quality beats quantity: a few hundred to a few thousand clean, reviewed examples are often enough for LoRA tasks.
  • Keep evaluation cases out of training data, or your scores become meaningless.
  • Watch for regressions: general ability, safety behaviour and other languages can degrade (catastrophic forgetting).
  • Respect model and data licenses. Some forbid using outputs to train other models.
  • Tools: Hugging Face PEFT and TRL, Unsloth, Axolotl, sentence-transformers. Serve LoRA adapters with vLLM or merge them into the base model.

6Common mistakes

  • Fine-tuning to "teach the documents" instead of using RAG.
  • Training on unreviewed, noisy model outputs.
  • No held-out evaluation, or leakage of test cases.
  • Changing the embedder without re-indexing.

8Check yourself

Your policies change monthly. Fine-tune or RAG?
RAG: re-index when documents change. Fine-tuned knowledge goes stale and can't cite sources.
What makes QLoRA cheaper than LoRA on a full-precision model?
The frozen base model is stored in 4-bit, cutting memory roughly 4×, so larger models fit on one GPU while only small adapters are trained.
Why fine-tune the embedder before the LLM in a RAG system?
Retrieval quality usually limits RAG. A domain-tuned retriever finds the right chunks; the LLM can only use what it is given.
