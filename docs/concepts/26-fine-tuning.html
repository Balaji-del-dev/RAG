1What is fine-tuning?

Interview answer — say this first
In simple words

Fine-tuning means taking a model that is already trained and training it a little more on your own examples, so it learns a new behaviour: a format, a style, a task. LoRA makes this cheap by training only small add-on parts. RLHF and DPO teach a model which answers people prefer.

2Why do we need it?

Fine-tuning is often suggested first and needed last. Done for the wrong reason (to "teach the documents"), it costs weeks and the knowledge goes out of date. Done for the right reason, it gives a smaller, cheaper model that follows your format perfectly, or a retriever that finally understands your company's jargon.

Fine-tuning is good for

  • Consistent format, style and tone
  • Classification, extraction and routing tasks
  • Reliable JSON or tool-call formats
  • Making a small model as good as a big one on your task
  • Domain-tuned embedders and rerankers

Fine-tuning is poor for

  • Adding facts that change (use RAG)
  • Citations and traceability
  • Per-user access control
  • Fixing bad retrieval
Remember — the adaptation ladder (climb only as far as needed)

1. Better prompts → 2. RAG for knowledge → 3. Better retrieval (chunking, hybrid, reranking) → 4. Fine-tune the embedder or reranker → 5. Fine-tune the LLM.

3How does it work?

How fine-tuning with LoRA works

1

Prepare training examples

Collect a few hundred to a few thousand clean, reviewed examples of input → ideal output. Quality matters more than quantity. Keep evaluation cases out of the training data.

2

Freeze the base model

The original weights are not changed. This keeps training cheap and avoids damaging the model.

3

Add and train small LoRA adapters

LoRA adds small "low-rank" matrices to some layers and trains only those — often under 1% of the parameters. QLoRA does the same on a 4-bit base model, so a 7–14B model can be tuned on one GPU.

4

Evaluate on held-out data

Test on examples the model never saw, and check that general ability, safety and other languages did not get worse (catastrophic forgetting).

5

Serve the adapter

Load the small adapter file on top of the base model (vLLM supports this), or merge it into the base model.

How preference tuning works: RLHF and DPO

RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

Model writes 2+ answers
People rank themwhich is better?
Train a reward modelpredicts the human preference
Reinforcement learning (PPO)model learns to get a high reward

DPO (Direct Preference Optimization)

Pairs: better vs worse answer
Train the model directlyraise the better, lower the worse — no reward model

RLHF and DPO are how chat models learn to be helpful and safe (lesson 01, stage 3). DPO is simpler and very popular.

4Example: step by step

Fine-tuning the retriever — the best payoff in RAG
  1. Collect (question, relevant chunk) pairs from evaluation cases, user clicks and reviewed answers — or generate synthetic questions for each chunk with a local LLM and review them.
  2. Find hard negatives: chunks the current retriever ranks high but that are wrong.
  3. Train the embedder with a contrastive loss (sentence-transformers makes this a short script); train the reranker on (question, passage, label).
  4. Evaluate on a held-out set: recall@k, MRR, nDCG.
  5. A new embedder means re-embedding everything into a new index generation; a new reranker only changes query-time behaviour.
Distillation: a small model that acts like a big one

A large model (check its license) produces high-quality outputs for 5,000 real invoice-extraction inputs. The outputs are reviewed, and a small 3B model is fine-tuned on them with LoRA. The small model now matches the large one on this task, at a fraction of the cost and latency.

5Important terms

Fine-tuning

Further training of a pre-trained model on task-specific examples.

SFT (supervised fine-tuning)

Fine-tuning on examples of input with the ideal output.

PEFT

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning: train only a small number of parameters.

LoRA

Low-Rank Adaptation: train small low-rank matrices added to a frozen model.

QLoRA

LoRA on a 4-bit quantized base model, so big models fit on one GPU.

RLHF

Train a reward model from human rankings, then optimize the model with reinforcement learning.

DPO

Direct Preference Optimization: learn from better/worse answer pairs without a reward model.

Distillation

Train a small "student" model to imitate a large "teacher" model.

Catastrophic forgetting

The model loses general abilities after narrow fine-tuning.

Hard negatives

Wrong passages that look relevant; they teach a retriever fine distinctions.

7Advantages and limitations

Advantages

  • Perfectly consistent format and style
  • Smaller, cheaper models for narrow tasks
  • Shorter prompts (no long instructions needed)
  • Retrievers that understand your jargon

Limitations

  • Needs good, reviewed training data
  • Knowledge goes out of date
  • Can cause catastrophic forgetting
  • Every change needs re-training and re-evaluation
  • License rules on model outputs and data

9How to explain it in an interview

Your 1-minute answer

DefinitionFine-tuning is the further training of a pre-trained model on a smaller, task-specific dataset so that it adapts its behaviour, such as output format, style or a specialized task.

HowParameter-efficient methods like LoRA freeze the base model and train small low-rank adapters, usually under one percent of the parameters; QLoRA does this on a 4-bit base model so it fits on one GPU. Preference tuning, with RLHF or DPO, teaches the model which answers people prefer.

WhenFor knowledge I use RAG, because fine-tuned knowledge goes stale and cannot be cited. I fine-tune for consistent formats, narrow tasks, distillation into smaller models, and especially for domain-specific embedders and rerankers.

RisksI use clean, reviewed data, keep evaluation cases out of training, and check for catastrophic forgetting and license restrictions.

Don't say"We fine-tune the LLM on our documents so it knows them."
Say instead"RAG for knowledge; fine-tuning for behaviour, format, cost — and often for the retriever."
Don't say"RLHF and fine-tuning are the same thing."
Say instead"SFT learns from ideal answers; RLHF and DPO learn from preferences between answers."
